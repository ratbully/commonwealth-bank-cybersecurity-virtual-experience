**Task 2: Cyber Security Incident Response**
**Commonwealth Bank Cybersecurity Virtual Experience**
**1. Introduction**

This task focuses on investigating and responding to a cyber security incident involving phishing emails, credential theft, and a suspected ransomware attack.

As a cyber security analyst, my responsibility is to identify the nature of the incident, assess its impact, recommend appropriate containment measures, support recovery, and identify preventive measures to reduce the risk of similar incidents in the future.

The scenario involves employees receiving fraudulent emails impersonating the Human Resources department. These emails direct employees to a fake company support portal designed to steal login credentials and distribute malicious software.

**2. Identification of the Cyber Attack**

The incident involves two major types of cyber attacks:

**A. Phishing Attack**

Phishing is a social engineering attack in which cybercriminals send fraudulent emails pretending to be legitimate organisations or trusted individuals to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.

In this scenario:

Employees received fraudulent emails impersonating the HR department.
The emails instructed employees to update their timesheets through a company support portal.
The link redirected employees to a fake website designed to look like the legitimate portal.
Employees who entered their credentials unknowingly exposed their usernames and passwords to attackers.

**B. Ransomware Attack**

Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts files or restricts access to systems and demands payment to restore access.

Evidence suggesting a ransomware infection includes:

Employees reporting that shared files were no longer accessible.
Users receiving errors when attempting to open previously accessible Word documents.
The suspicious emails were also found to distribute a harmful program.

The inability to access shared files and documents suggests that ransomware may have infected systems within the organisation.

Conclusion: The incident appears to involve a phishing campaign that led to credential theft and potentially introduced ransomware into the organisation's network.

**3. Immediate Next Steps as a Cyber Security Analyst**

As a cyber security analyst, I would take the following actions:

Report and escalate the incident: Immediately notify the incident response team, IT security management, and relevant stakeholders.
Confirm the scope of the incident: Identify affected employees, devices, accounts, systems, and shared file locations.
Preserve evidence: Secure suspicious emails, email headers, system logs, authentication records, and relevant security alerts for investigation.
Identify compromised accounts: Determine which employees entered their credentials into the fraudulent website.
Investigate affected devices: Examine computers and systems for signs of malware or ransomware infection.
Review network activity: Analyse network traffic and security logs to identify suspicious connections and possible malware communication.
Block malicious indicators: Block confirmed phishing domains, malicious URLs, file hashes, and other identified indicators of compromise.
Notify affected employees: Inform employees about the phishing campaign and instruct them not to open suspicious emails or attachments.
Protect sensitive information: Assess whether confidential employee or banking information may have been accessed or exposed.
Maintain incident documentation: Record the timeline, affected systems, investigation findings, and response actions.

**4. Containment, Resolution and Recovery**

**A. Containment**

The primary objective is to prevent the incident from spreading and causing additional damage.

Actions include:

Isolate infected computers from the corporate network.
Disable or temporarily suspend compromised user accounts.
Reset passwords for affected accounts using trusted procedures.
Revoke active sessions and authentication tokens associated with compromised accounts.
Block malicious email addresses, URLs, and domains.
Remove the phishing emails from employee inboxes where possible.
Restrict access to affected shared drives and network resources.
Apply additional monitoring to detect suspicious activity.
Preserve forensic evidence before rebuilding affected systems.
**B. Resolution**

The objective is to remove the threat and restore the organisation's security.

Actions include:

Conduct a thorough investigation to determine the source and extent of the infection.
Identify and remove malicious software from affected systems.
Reimage compromised computers where necessary using trusted system images.
Patch vulnerable operating systems and applications.
Investigate how the attackers gained access to the environment.
Review and strengthen email security controls.
Verify that compromised accounts and systems are no longer under attacker control.
Confirm that no additional malicious activity remains in the network.
**C. Recovery**

The objective is to restore normal business operations safely.

Actions include:

Restore encrypted or inaccessible files from clean, verified backups.
Confirm that backup files are free from malware before restoration.
Restore affected systems using trusted recovery procedures.
Reconnect recovered devices to the network only after security checks.
Reset credentials and enforce multi-factor authentication where possible.
Monitor systems for unusual activity after recovery.
Confirm that employees can safely access shared files and business applications.
Obtain confirmation from relevant departments that normal operations have resumed.
**5. Post-Incident Activities**

After resolving the incident, the organisation should perform the following activities:

Conduct a lessons-learned review: Evaluate how the incident occurred, how it was detected, and how effectively the response team handled it.
Document the incident: Prepare a detailed report containing the timeline, attack methods, affected systems, impact, and actions taken.
Review security controls: Assess existing email filtering, endpoint protection, access management, and network security measures.
Provide employee security awareness training: Educate employees on recognising phishing emails, suspicious links, malicious attachments, and social engineering techniques.
Implement multi-factor authentication: Strengthen account security by requiring additional verification during login.
Improve backup procedures: Maintain regular, encrypted, offline or otherwise isolated backups and test restoration procedures.
Update incident response procedures: Improve response plans based on lessons learned from the incident.
Strengthen monitoring and detection: Use security monitoring tools to identify unusual login attempts, suspicious network activity, and possible ransomware behaviour.
Review access permissions: Apply the principle of least privilege so employees only have access to the information and systems required for their roles.
Report and communicate appropriately: Provide relevant updates to management and follow applicable regulatory and internal reporting requirements.
**6. Preventive Measures and Recommendations**

To reduce the likelihood of similar incidents in the future, I recommend:

Conduct regular phishing awareness and simulation exercises.
Implement advanced email filtering and anti-phishing protection.
Enforce multi-factor authentication across business systems.
Maintain updated antivirus and endpoint detection solutions.
Regularly patch operating systems and applications.
Use network segmentation to limit the spread of malware.
Maintain secure and regularly tested backups.
Monitor unusual account activity and unauthorised access attempts.
Establish clear procedures for reporting suspicious emails.
Conduct regular security assessments and incident response exercises.
**7. Conclusion**

This incident demonstrates how a phishing email can become the starting point of a much larger cyber security incident.

The attackers used a fraudulent HR communication to trick employees into providing their login credentials and potentially downloading malicious software. The subsequent loss of access to shared files and Word documents suggests a possible ransomware infection.

As a cyber security analyst, the most important priorities are to identify the affected systems, contain the threat, protect sensitive information, remove malicious software, and safely restore business operations.

The incident also highlights the importance of employee awareness, strong authentication, effective security monitoring, and reliable backup procedures.

By implementing these recommendations, Commonwealth Bank can strengthen its security posture and reduce the risk and impact of similar cyber attacks in the future.
