**Incident Timeline and Analysis
Commonwealth Bank Cybersecurity Virtual Experience**

**Task: 02 – Cyber Security Incident Response**

**1. Incident Overview**

This document provides a chronological analysis of the cyber security incident involving a phishing campaign, credential theft, and suspected ransomware infection.

The incident affected employees within the Risk Department and potentially compromised access to company systems and shared files.

**2. Incident Timeline**

Time	Incident/Event	Security Analysis
10:30 a.m.	An employee reports receiving a suspicious email impersonating the HR department.	Initial indication of a phishing attack.
10:30 a.m.	The employee clicks a link directing them to a fake company support portal and enters their login credentials.	Possible credential theft and exposure of employee account information.
2:00 p.m.	Eight additional reports of similar phishing emails are received by the IT Service Desk.	Indicates a wider phishing campaign targeting employees.
Investigation	Security teams discover that 62 employees in the Risk Department received similar emails over two days.	Confirms the campaign affected multiple employees and requires investigation of its scope.
Investigation	The fraudulent website is found to be designed to steal usernames and passwords and download harmful software.	Indicates both credential harvesting and potential malware distribution.
3:50 p.m.	Employees report that shared files are inaccessible and Word documents display errors when opened.	Possible ransomware infection or file corruption affecting business operations.
**3. Key Security Findings**

The timeline highlights several important security concerns:

**1. Phishing and Social Engineering**

Attackers impersonated the HR department and used a timesheet approval request to encourage employees to click a malicious link.

**2. Credential Compromise**

An employee entered their username and password into a fraudulent website, potentially exposing their account to unauthorised access.

**3. Widespread Targeting**

A total of 62 employees in the Risk Department received similar emails, indicating a coordinated phishing campaign.

**4. Malware Distribution**

The fraudulent website was designed to download harmful software, creating a potential entry point for malware.

**5. Possible Ransomware Infection**

The inability to access shared files and open Word documents suggests possible ransomware activity. Further investigation would be required to confirm encryption and determine the extent of the damage.

**6. Incident Impact Assessment**

The incident could have affected the organisation in several ways:

Confidentiality: Employee credentials and potentially sensitive organisational information may have been exposed.
Integrity: Files may have been modified, encrypted, or corrupted by malicious software.
Availability: Employees experienced difficulties accessing shared files and business documents.
Business operations: The incident could disrupt departmental activities and normal banking operations.
Security risk: Compromised credentials could potentially be used to access additional company systems.

**7. Priority Response Actions**

Based on the incident timeline, the following actions should be prioritised:

Investigate and isolate potentially infected devices.
Identify employees who entered their credentials into the fraudulent website.
Disable or secure compromised accounts and revoke active sessions.
Block the malicious website and associated indicators of compromise.
Preserve suspicious emails, system logs, and other forensic evidence.
Investigate affected shared drives and network resources.
Determine whether ransomware has encrypted files or spread to other systems.
Restore affected files from verified clean backups after removing the threat.

**8. Conclusion**

The incident timeline demonstrates how a phishing campaign can develop into a more serious cyber security incident.
The initial fraudulent HR email resulted in potential credential theft, while the subsequent reports of inaccessible shared files raised concerns about a possible ransomware infection.
Early detection, effective incident reporting, rapid containment, and coordinated recovery are essential to limiting the damage.
This incident highlights the importance of employee security awareness, strong authentication controls, effective email security, and regular data backups.
